# Phase 9 — Beta polish, economy clarity & long-career reliability

Scope: the four limitations recorded in `docs/BETA_READINESS.md`. No game rule, price, purse or probability was changed. Every simulation change is performance-only and was proven identical (below).

## 1. Financial clarity before committing to a show
**Root cause.** The forecast already held the right ranges, but the page showed only totals (revenue, costs, profit) and one generic warning ("smaller venue, cheaper purses or better pricing could help" — which promised more than the model could back). A player could not see that £49.5k of purses and £9k of travel were being set against a hall whose whole gate was £16.8k.

**Change.** `engine/eventEconomics.ts` (public information only) breaks the existing forecast into the lines settlement actually posts — venue hire, fighter purses, production, broadcast production, officials/medical, travel, marketing, security (per head), sanctioning levy (10 % of tickets), win bonuses — each tagged *owed whatever the crowd does* / *grows with the crowd* / *depends on results*, with income lines, break-even tickets, the full-house result, the average ticket a sell-out would need, and cash after the poor case (the same figure the advisor quotes; a test asserts they are equal). When a loss is forecast the page says why (best case, sell-out arithmetic, biggest cost drivers, break-even against the forecast crowd) and names only controls that exist: ticket prices (editable, forecast re-calculates), another hall (only if its forecast is genuinely better, with the real cancellation refund rule: 50 % of hire if ≥ 8 weeks out, otherwise nothing, agreed fights return to the list), purses (fixed by the negotiated terms; fights can come off the card), sponsor offers present, local TV only if its fee exceeds its production cost. It never says a lever will make the show profitable, and a show forecast to profit gets no loss text. Putting a forecast-loss show on sale now asks first, showing owed money, forecast result, break-even and cash at risk. Advisor wording corrected.

**Forecast vs settlement (tests: `phase9.test.ts`).** Sold-out small hall (450/450), partially attended (Hull Arena) and badly attended (priced 4× the default, 222–620 of 3,200 seats) shows are built, forecast, played and settled: venue, purses, production and broadcast production equal the settlement exactly; security equals attendance × £1.10 and officials + travel + levy equal the settled "officials" line within £2 (each posting is rounded to the pound); the settled profit equals revenue − every cost line; a sell-out without sponsor/broadcast lands on the forecast's full-house figure to within the win bonuses paid; the reported case (sold out, still loses) is flagged "beyond a sell-out" before sale and then loses; break-even is arithmetic (profit at that crowd ≈ 0 within one ticket).
Accuracy of the ranges (reference bot, 7 careers × 3 years, 215 shows, `scripts/audit/phase9-forecast-accuracy.ts`): actual profit inside the forecast range **86.5 %**; actual attendance inside the range **74.9 %**; where the forecast called the *sign* (whole range loss or whole range profit) it was right **153 of 154** times; mean absolute error of the midpoint profit £54.6k (shows range from £80k loss to £230k profit). The ranges are honest rather than tight, and the page says so.

## 2. Performance gate
**Findings.** (a) The gate compared one 8-segment run against `structuredClone` of a small world. Controlled repeated runs showed single-run ratios of 6.2–9.6× (reference clone 2.6–6.4 ms between runs) on an unchanged build, so one run was mostly host noise; the Phase 6.5 code (the baseline) read 7.2–11.0× the same way. (b) Profiling an eight-year passive career by time window: `cloneState` grew from 20 % to 27 % of a tick and GC from 16 % to 18 % — the weekly deep copy of ever-growing finished history; `pickVenue`/`venueBookedOn` (a scan of all events for each of ~100 venues) grew 2.5 → 5.1 %. Nothing else grew.
**Fixes (performance-only).** `venuesBookedOn` computes the day's booked venues in one pass. `cloneForTick` (clone.ts) *shares* settled fights (> 120 days after processing) and archived events between the old and new state instead of copying them; the two places that edited such records in place now replace them (copy-on-write). Proof of invisibility: **golden fingerprints** (`scripts/audit/phase9-golden.ts`: SHA-256 of the canonical state at 6 checkpoints × 3 passive careers up to 5 years + a 3-year bot career) are **identical** to commit f9e36e3; a test freezes every shared record and plays 6-year/3-year careers (nothing edits history); a test plays four years week by week with and without sharing and compares the full states; a purity test shows the input state is unchanged.
**Measured (4 interleaved process runs each, baseline f9e36e3 vs this tree; median tick ms; `scripts/audit/results/phase9-perf-distribution.txt`).** Fresh world: 26.0 / 27.5 / 25.8 / 27.4 → 23.6 / 22.8 / 23.0 / 23.5 (**−13 %**). Six-year-old world (3.4 MB JSON, 1,276 fights, same state every run): 47.3 / 48.4 / 49.2 / 48.2 → 34.4 / 36.0 / 30.2 / 36.0 (**−29 %**).
**Methodology.** The limit is still 10×. The decision is now the **median of 5 independent repetitions' medians** (`gateDecision`, benchTime.ts), and the distribution is printed. Noise only adds time and rarely moves most repetitions; a real slowdown multiplies every one. Measured on this host, statistic (min–max of repetitions): baseline 8.05 (7.91–9.63), 8.75 (8.02–10.74), 8.11 (7.18–9.81), 8.85 (7.78–9.94); this tree 7.65 (7.03–9.48), 7.28 (6.76–8.44), 7.57 (6.58–9.26), 7.68 (7.30–10.30), 7.29 (6.79–8.76) — individual repetitions cross 10 sometimes, the statistic never did. A second test injects +60 % work per tick and requires the statistic to rise ≥ 1.35× (measured 1.57–1.81×, i.e. ≈ 12.3–13.0 > 10: a real slowdown fails); `perfgate.test.ts` checks the rule on synthetic data (healthy-noisy passes, +45 % fails, one wild repetition cannot flip it).

## 3. Long-career save growth
Audit (`phase9-savesize.ts`, JSON / gzip, which is what IndexedDB stores):
| year | passive (From the Ground Up) | busy bot (Regional) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 1.06 MB / 175 kB | 1.26 MB / 208 kB |
| 1 | 1.42 / 228 | 1.71 / 275 |
| 2 | 2.05 / 307 | 2.39 / 373 |
| 4 | 2.91 / 416 | 3.55 / 539 |
| 6 | 3.26 / 458 | 4.25 / 643 |
| 8 | 3.39 / 474 | 4.70 / 706 |
Growth is bounded for the world (passive: +10 % over years 5–8; fighters stay ≈ 400, retirees pruned) and linear only in the player's own activity (each show and fight the player is involved in is kept: ≈ +0.45 MB JSON / +65 kB gz per busy year). Compressed size is ~14–15 % of JSON.
What may and may not go: kept by design — fighter records and profiles, every fight the player is involved in with scorecards, rankings and reign history, ledger (folded into `ledgerArchive`, cash reconciles), media/awards history, active events; already pruned — AI fights > 3 years old no fighter lists in their last six, retired fighters nobody refers to, old rival events, cancelled events. Of the remaining 1.2–1.9 k fights ≈ 18 % are > 3 years old but are in some fighter's recent-fights list, i.e. visible profile history — not removable.
**Compaction was measured and rejected.** The only dead, derivable data is the camp-prep, terms, city and country of settled AI fights (36 % of fight bytes, mostly `sideA/B.prep`): dropping it from all 1,049 eligible fights of a six-year world saves 10.9 % of JSON but only **4.3 % of the compressed save** (466 → 446 kB), because identical objects compress away — not worth a schema change and a migration. No history was deleted.
**What did change for storage.** The localStorage fallback (private windows, no IndexedDB; ~5 MB for the whole origin) stored plain JSON, so a six-year career (3–5 MB) plus its autosave slot could not fit and saves failed. It now stores `gz64:` + base64 of the same gzip (≈ 0.9 MB for the 4.7 MB career) and still reads old plain slots. Tests: a six-year career is saved into a 1.5 MB Storage (the JSON alone is ~3 MB), reloads identical, and a legacy plain slot still loads. In memory, sharing finished history also removes it from the weekly copy and GC (section 2).

## 4. Inbox and mobile notifications
**Audit** (regional career, 26 weeks, 42 messages unread): ten "X wants a fight" nudges (one per idle fighter), nine media requests (three-week expiry), scouting tips, ranking updates, rival offers and withdrawals — most informational, a minority decisions.
**Changes** (`engine/inboxViews.ts`, derived — nothing stored, deleted or hidden; old saves classify the same way): every message is Needs action / Results / Championship / Updates. A decision is *open* only while the thing it asks about is (rival offer still open, media request open, sponsor offer unexpired, fighter still idle and unsigned-to-a-fight); money warnings and contract warnings are always actionable and never auto-closed; mandatory-challenge and title news is Championship. The inbox shows "N waiting for a decision" in its head, pins open decisions first, offers five wrapping priority tabs with unread counts (open count on Needs action), folds runs of three or more similar messages (Ranking updates ×4, Fighters asking for a fight ×10…) into one expandable row with its unread/open counts — every message stays reachable — says on a message whether its decision is still open or closed (the message stays), and adds "Mark these read" for the current view. Test: classify/fold lose and invent nothing; an offer is a decision while open and a record afterwards; mandatory challenges classify as championship; an idle-fighter nudge stops being a decision once they are booked.
**Toasts.** At most two at once; a warning (7 s) is never pushed off by a note (4 s); message counts from quick successive weeks add up in one notice ("11 new messages") instead of stacking; on a phone a toast is a one-line note above the bottom bar, never intercepts a tap (`pointer-events: none`), only the newest note shows (a warning is kept), and respects the safe-area insets.

## Known limitations
- The break-even uses the forecast price mix and low-end sponsor/broadcast income; real crowds miss the forecast range about 1 time in 8 on profit and 1 in 4 on attendance.
- A player who keeps every fight and event keeps ≈ 65 kB gz per busy year; a 20-year career is ≈ 1.5 MB compressed. No archival of the player's own history exists (deliberate).
- The first-show economics are unchanged: default fight terms still lose money in a small hall; the page now explains why and what exists to change it.
- The relative perf gate remains host-dependent; it now fails only on a sustained slowdown (about +30–35 % over today's median), not on a noisy minute.
- Folded inbox rows hide individual messages until expanded; unread counts are shown on the row.
